05 / Beyond the warranty

Built for days.
Worked for years.

Mission teams design for demanding environments and limited resources. Some explorers then operate far beyond their planned lifetimes — not by magic, but through robust engineering, careful operations and scientific teams that adapt.

Sojourner · 19977 → 83 sols · nearly 12×
Opportunity · 2004–201890 → 5,111 sols · nearly 57×
Ingenuity · 2021–2024Up to 5 planned → 72 flights

Orange: original design target. Cyan: achieved operational measure. A sol is one Martian day, about 24 h 39 min. Flight count is used for Ingenuity, not duration.

1966
Surveyor 1

Made the first successful U.S. soft landing on the Moon and returned more than 11,000 images.

1969
ALSEP

Apollo crews left automated science stations that continued sending lunar measurements until 1977.

1971
Lunar Roving Vehicle

Three battery-powered rovers were deliberately parked at their Apollo landing sites after their final drives.

1997
Sojourner

Outlasted its planned 7 sols and demonstrated practical rover operations on Mars.

2010–11
Spirit

After becoming stuck in soft soil, it fell silent; NASA ended contact attempts in 2011.

2018
Opportunity / Dawn

Dust ended Opportunity’s communications; depleted hydrazine ended Dawn’s ability to point and communicate.

2022
InSight

Dust accumulation reduced solar power until the lander could no longer maintain operations.

2024
Ingenuity

After 72 flights, rotor-blade damage ended the first powered, controlled flight campaign on another planet.
